1 Introduction
Tool-using language models interleave reasoning with ac-
tions, learn tool-use behavior through prompting or self-
supervision,andoperateoverlargeAPIcollections(Yaoetal.
2023;Schicketal.2023;Patiletal.2024).Inrealisticdeploy-
ments,however,theyrarelyactonapristineprompt.Instead,
they consume an accumulating interaction record in which
users revise requests, tools return partial or failed observa-
tions, and earlier turns leave behind identifiers, arguments,
andstateclaims.Suchinformationmayremainsyntactically
valid and semantically plausible after it has stopped govern-
ing the current action.
Figure 1A illustrates this failure. An airline assistant has
the evidence needed to update reservationKKKYCG, but the
history also contains a failed tool call using the invalid ar-
gument namepaymentId. Because that failure does not
changethereservationstate,thecorrectnextactionisidenti-
cal with or without the trace. A robust model should follow
the current schema and emitpayment_id; instead, it may
imitate the misleading historical precedent. Similar failures
arisefromtracesinvolvinganotherentity,unsupportedcom-
pletion claims, or tools that are no longer available.
Existingtool-usebenchmarksspanexecutableAPIsuites,
large-scaleAPIcollections,functionselectionandargument
generation, stateful interaction, and when-to-call behavior
(Li et al. 2023; Qin et al. 2024; Patil et al. 2025; Liu et al.
2025; Prabhakar et al. 2026; Lu et al. 2025; Yao et al. 2025;
Ross, Mahabaleshwarkar, and Suhara 2025). Recent robust-
nessworkstudiesnaturalqueryvariationsandchangestothe
available toolkit (Rabinovich and Anaby Tavor 2025), while
ReflecTool-Bench evaluates whether models detect and re-
pairpriortool-usemistakesinmulti-turndialogues(Liuetal.
2026). These settings do not isolate whether a plausible but
non-authoritative historical trace redirects the model when
the current request, available tools, and gold action remain
fixed. Consequently, aggregate errors cannot distinguish a
model thatlacks the required policyfrom one thatpossesses
it under reliable context but is redirected by misleading his-
tory.
We study this failure mode through a controlled interven-
tion. For each decision point, we construct three synchro-
nized views: the untouched Original trajectory, a Polluted
trajectory containing an inserted misleading trace, and an
Oracle State view containing only reliable task state. The
system policy, tool set, latest request, and gold next action
remain identical across views. This paired design directly
tests whether misleading history flips a decision that the
model can otherwise execute correctly.
The effect is substantial. On Qwen3-1.7B (Yang et al.
2025), pollution flips32.14%of examples that are correct
under Original history, affecting bothCallandAnswer
decisions;directreuseofinjectedentitiesandinterfaceforms
further identifies the misleading trace as the cause. We call
this phenomenon history-induced policy hijacking.
To measure and mitigate it, we introduceContext
Pollute-Bench(Figure 1B), which pairs synchronized
Original,Polluted,andOracleStateviewsunderelevengold-
preserving interventions. We further propose Oracle-guided

2 Related Work
Tool-useevaluationandrobustness.Tool-usinglanguage
models have been studied through reasoning–action frame-
works, self-supervised tool use, and large-scale API access
(Yao et al. 2023; Schick et al. 2023; Patil et al. 2024). Ex-
isting benchmarks cover executable APIs and training re-
sources (Li et al. 2023; Qin et al. 2024), function and argu-
ment correctness (Patil et al. 2025; Liu et al. 2025), stateful
interaction(Luetal.2025;Yaoetal.2025),verifiablemulti-
turn trajectories (Prabhakar et al. 2026), and when-to-call
behavior (Ross, Mahabaleshwarkar, and Suhara 2025). Ro-
bustness work varies queries or available tools (Rabinovich
and Anaby Tavor 2025); ReflecTool-Bench evaluates repair
of prior mistakes (Liu et al. 2026); and InjecAgent and
AgentDojostudymaliciousinstructionsintoolcontent(Zhan
et al. 2024; Debenedetti et al. 2024). In contrast,Context
Pollute-Benchkeeps the current request, tools, and gold
action fixed and intervenes only on non-authoritative his-
tory, isolating policy preservation rather than task variation,
error repair, or instruction injection.
Context and policy distillation.Multi-turn presentation
can alter model behavior even when task information is pre-
served (Laban et al. 2026; Chen, Wu, and Leskovec 2026).
Knowledge and sequence-level distillation transfer teacher
distributionsordecodedoutputs(Hinton,Vinyals,andDean
2015;KimandRush2016),whilereverse-KLandon-policy
distillation supervise generative students on their visited
states (Gu et al. 2024; Agarwal et al. 2024). Learning from
privilegedinformationandcontextdistillationstudysupervi-
sionavailableduringtrainingbutabsentatdeployment(Vap-
nikandVashist2009;Lopez-Pazetal.2016;Snell,Klein,and
Zhong 2022; Ye et al. 2026; Penaloza et al. 2026).Oracle-
OPDspecializes these principles to reliability-asymmetric
tool use, with an Oracle-conditioned teacher supervising a
student that operates on potentially misleading histories.

3.2 Oracle-Guided On-Policy Distillation
DesignOracle-OPDcombines three components moti-
vated by the benchmark diagnosis.
Reliable-State Conditioning.The teacher conditions on
the synchronized Oracle State, representing the policy in-
duced by authoritative task evidence rather than by compet-
ing historical traces.
Distributional Policy Transfer.Token-level soft targets
preserve the teacher’s relative preferences over answering,
calling, tool selection, and argument generation, which are
discarded by a single hard target sequence.

generatedprefixmaynotcoverthestatesreachedbyastudent
conditioned on misleading history.Oracle-OPDtherefore
