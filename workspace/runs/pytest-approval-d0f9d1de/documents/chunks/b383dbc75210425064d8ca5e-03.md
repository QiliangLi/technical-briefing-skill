4.4 Strategy Selector
We next evaluate the energy model in isolation: how well it ranks
strategies, whether the energy formulation helps over a plain clas-
sifier, and which structural features drive its decisions.
Training cost.Fitting the selector is a negligible one-time cost. The
model has only15 .9K parameters, and training on the full corpus
of400matrices for600epochs takes about two seconds on a single
GPU. There is no overlap between this training set and our test set
for main results in Section 4.1. Because training is offline, its cost is
amortized over every matrix and kernel the selector subsequently
guides, and extending the corpus with new measurements retrains it
at the same negligible cost. At inference the model ranks a matrix’s
strategies in well under a microsecond, negligible beside feature
extraction and the downstream code generation, so the selector
adds no measurable overhead to the generation pipeline.
Ranking quality.We rank the strategies using an offline corpus of
400SuiteSparse matrices with measured runtimes for every strat-
egy. The energy model outperforms a multilayer perceptron trained
with the same listwise objective on all three operators, with the
largest gains on SpMM and SpGEMM. SpMV is nearly saturated,
as all models recover most of the oracle speedup and candidate
strategies differ only marginally. The proposed interpretable model
also remains competitive with gradient-boosted trees [9]. Full pro-
tocols, metric definitions, and per-operator results are provided in
the supplementary material.
Which features matter.Because the energy is additive, each rec-
ommendation decomposes into per-feature energy contributions,
giving a direct, model-native read on which structural features
drive the ranking (Figure 7). Rather than hinging on a single fea-
ture, the model draws on a broad set of correlated structural signals.
Features like matrix density, nonzero count, and row count are
consistently influential across all three operators, with operator-
specific signals layered on top: the dense width 𝐾 for SpMM, and
accumulator-demand statistics (intermediate-product and 𝐴𝐴T up-
per bounds) for SpGEMM. The full per-feature breakdown is given
in the supplementary material. All features are computed from
bounded scans at negligible cost, and which of them matter is input
and operator-specific.

4.6 End-to-End Application Evaluation
Following DTC-SpMM’s end-to-end protocol [7], we evaluate the

reuse the corresponding forward kernel. The generated implemen-

5 Conclusion
GPU sparse kernels require the data format, execution schedule,
and hardware mapping to match the sparsity pattern, and our char-
acterization shows that a mismatch costs orders of magnitude while
no fixed implementation dominates. SparseDitto answers this by
treating the workload as the data: it profiles the input, ranks es-
tablished strategies with a glass-box additive model, and has LLM
agents plan and implement several candidates that measurements
on the target GPU refine and validate, under one design frame-
work covering SpMV, SpMM, and SpGEMM. Against cuSPARSE it
achieves a2 .68× geometric-mean speedup on an RTX PRO 6000
and2 .79× on an H200, it is1 .57×–5.37× faster than four SOTA
specialized systems on matched inputs, and it accelerates full-batch
GCN training by up to3.39×.
