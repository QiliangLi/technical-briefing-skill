## 1. Background: The AI era's paradigm shift from piling up computing power to enhancing it with networking

A new era for computing power: The evolving role of networking

In the AI-driven digital economy, computing power has become a new form of productivity. As large language models (LLMs) exceed trillions of parameters, industries are rapidly building AI computing centers. However, an often-overlooked challenge is emerging: AI training and inference efficiency depends not only on peak computing power but also on how efficiently data flows from storage to computation. As computing resources become increasingly abundant, the network is no longer merely a link between compute nodes—it is a critical lever for improving overall computing efficiency.

The root cause of the computing power bottleneck: Network inefficiency

AI model training and inference have disrupted traditional IT architectures, which rely primarily on symmetrical north-south traffic between users and servers. Networks designed for those architectures now constrain computing performance:

- Surge in east-west traffic: Increased pressure on data center internal networks Distributed training relies on high-frequency communication between GPU clusters—for example, AllReduce—causing east-west traffic to rise from 20% to 80% of total traffic. During LLM training, communication time accounts for over 30% of total training time, making the network an “invisible bottleneck.” Data center networks must improve communication efficiency using features such as lossless transmission, ultra-high throughput, and RDMA over Converged Ethernet (RoCE).

### 2.2. Hybrid cloud network upgrades: 400 Gbps low-latency access over a single leased line, differentiated services, and hitless failover

Build a cross-cloud computing power pool with a 400 Gbps single-port leased line

When building a cross-cloud computing power pool with leased lines, bursty traffic—such as during data pulling—can occur. Previously, expanding bandwidth using multiple leased lines or ports in an Equal-Cost Multi-Path (ECMP) group often caused packet loss for bursty traffic due to uneven hashing across links—reducing computing efficiency. With a single port or leased line supporting 400 Gbps, this issue is significantly mitigated.

Egress QoS for leased lines ensures no interference between mixed services

In AI application scenarios, a cross-cloud computing cluster built with a single leased line may occasionally transmit offline training data. This creates elephant flows that consume the cluster's bandwidth and reduce its computing capacity. Leased line QoS can set different bandwidth ratios for services with different priorities, preventing interference and unexpected bandwidth consumption. By reasonably allocating bandwidth ratios and rate limits, it improves bandwidth utilization and avoids resource waste.

Failover groups enable millisecond-level convergence for leased line faults, ensuring service continuity

Leased line quality degradation and external environmental interference make interruptions unpredictable and unavoidable. Therefore, the route convergence speed of redundant leased lines is critical to business stability.

For a normal redundant leased line connection, when one line is interrupted, the route controller of the Apsara Luoshen Cloud Network Management platform detects the fault, switches the route, and recovers the service. With Border Gateway Protocol (BGP) Fast Detection (BFD) convergence technology, the entire switchover time is at the second level, typically between 1 and 10 seconds. Leased line failover group - Data plane fault escape: A disaster recovery tunnel is established between Virtual Border Routers (VBRs) in the same failover group. When a leased line is interrupted, the VBR of the interrupted line forwards outbound traffic to other VBRs in the same group, which then exits the cloud through other leased lines. The switchover time is less than 100 ms.
