broadcast. This way, we amortize SRAM accesses across multiple

6.3 Encoder and Decoder
To further reduce off-chip traffic,Deltorisdesigns a lightweight
encoder/decoder module that compresses data before it is writ-
ten back to DRAM. Our encoder design leverages the temporal
similarity of intermediate data across consecutive frames. Specifi-
cally, we compress the first frame and model weights using vector
quantization [56] to achieve high compression efficiency, while sub-
sequent frames are encoded via a lightweight compressed sparse
row (CSR) format to capture the sparse differences between frames.
This hybrid compression scheme can further reduce the data com-
munication overhead of intermediate data and model weights. Both
compression and decompression are pipelined with computation
and data fetching. Thus, it does not increase the overall latency.

theDeltorishardware. The hardware design is based on a 1D
systolic array architecture in Sec. 6.2.Deltorisconsists of 128

8.5 Scenarios with Fast Motion
To stress-test our technique, we further evaluate Deltoris under
rapid-motion scenarios. Since existing robotic VLA datasets do not
specifically target rapid robot motions, we construct a fast-motion
dataset by skipping intermediate observations during normal robot
execution while allowing the robot to execute additional steps.
This increases the change between consecutive observations and
effectively simulates faster robot motion.
In Fig. 24, we evaluate bothDeltoris and the baseline algorithm
on the PAD model and measure their performance and task accuracy.
The performance numbers are normalized to the GPU baseline. The
results show that, when the scene is accelerated by 2×, Deltoris
maintains accuracy comparable to the baseline, with less than 1%
accuracy loss. Further increasing the motion speed may degrade
the accuracy of Deltoris, as the temporal similarity between con-
secutive observations becomes weaker. Meanwhile, accelerating
the simulation scene would lead to modest performance degrada-
tion. The speedup decreases from 34.8× to 33.4× when the scene
is accelerated by 2×. Nevertheless, the results show that Deltoris
remains robust under fast-motion scenarios.
9 Related Work
Acceleration for Robotics.Over the past decade, computer archi-
tecture researchers have proposed a wide range of accelerators and
systems for robotics workloads [3, 4, 9, 13, 29, 30, 37, 38, 48, 62, 67–
69, 72, 75, 76, 83, 85, 100, 107]. Early efforts largely focused on
accelerating individual robotics kernels, such as model predictive
control (MPC) [3, 72], rigid-body dynamics [100], localization [62],
and collision detection [ 75, 76], each of which exposes distinct
domain-specific optimization opportunities. A second class of work,
including RoSÉ [69], Orianna [30], and AutoPilot [48], emphasizes
system-level optimization by exploiting common bottlenecks from
multiple algorithms, rather than kernel-specific acceleration. Tar-
tan [4], which is a CPU-centric robotic processor, also falls into this
category. More recently, architectural research has begun shifting
toward embodied AI [38, 85] and autonomous driving [9, 13], where
performance is governed not by a single computational kernel, but
by the end-to-end interaction among perception and control.
Unlike prior robotics accelerators that focus on classical controls,
Deltorisis the first focusing ondiffusion-based VLA inference, an
emerging embodied-AI workload with new computational char-
acteristics.Deltorisis designed to exploit the strong temporal
similarity across consecutive control steps in order to reduce both
computation and data communication overhead during inference.
Bit Sparsity.A large body of prior work [ 2, 11, 17, 42, 64, 77–
79, 89] has proposed bit-serial accelerators to eliminate ineffective
bit operations in DNN inference. Stripes [ 42] first showed that
bit-serial execution can scale performance with operand precision,
while Bit Fusion [78] proposed a fine-grained mixed-precision archi-
tecture. Pragmatic [2] and Laconic [77] further improved efficiency
by processing only effective terms. Subsequent works such as Bit-
let [64] and BitWave [79] introduced various mechanisms to im-
prove PE utilization. More recently, BBS [11] explored bidirectional
bit sparsity and approximation to further reduce computation.
However, these designs primarily target weight sparsity or static
bit-level inefficiency and do not exploit the temporal redundancy
inherent in diffusion-based VLA workloads. In contrast,Deltoris
is specifically designed to leverage temporal similarity across con-
secutive control steps and exposes much higher bit-level sparsity.

topic in architecture research. For example, both AdapTiV [102] and
HeatViT [18] reduce the computational overhead of ViTs through
adaptive token pruning. ViTALiTy [16] and ViTCoD [103], in con-
trast, target another key bottleneck in ViTs, the attention com-
putation, via sparse approximation. Recent work has also started
to explore diffusion-model acceleration. Cambricon-D [ 46] pro-
poses techniques to mitigate the memory overhead of differential
